# `/me init` interview

Ask one question at a time and keep each question short. Skip a question when the samples already answer it. Paraphrase each answer back only when it is ambiguous.

## Samples (always first)

> Paste 2–5 things you've actually written, the more varied the better: a Slack message, an email, a PR description, a post. Messy is fine. Real writing tells me more than any answer will.

If they paste only one kind of thing, ask once for a different kind.

## Core questions

1. **Where you write.** Where do you write most: Slack or Teams, email, GitHub, social, docs? Which of those do you want help with?
2. **Three words.** Pick three words for how you want to come across, and one word for how you *never* want to sound.
3. **Mechanics.** Quick ones. Which spelling do you use (US, UK or another)? Do you capitalise in chat? Use emoji? Em dashes? Exclamation marks?
4. **Openers and sign-offs.** How do you usually start and end an email, and a Slack message? ("hey", no greeting, "Cheers", nothing?)
5. **Pet peeves.** Which words or phrases make you cringe when AI writes them for you?
6. **Pushing back.** How do you say no, or disagree with someone senior? Give an example if you can.

## Optional (ask only if the user wants to go deeper)

7. **Humour.** Dry, silly, none? Where is it OK?
8. **Structure.** Bullets or paragraphs? Headers in docs? How long is too long?
9. **Hedging.** When you're unsure, what do you say? ("I think", "probably", "not 100% sure but")
10. **People.** Is there anyone whose writing you'd like to sound more like, or less like?
11. **Languages.** Do you mix in another language, slang, or local expressions? Where?

## Closing

After drafting, write two short test pieces in different registers, for example:

- A Slack reply declining a meeting.
- A short email asking a stranger for something.

Ask: "Does this sound like you? What would you change?" Put their corrections straight into the profile.
