# AI tells

These patterns make writing read as machine-generated. Remove them unless the user's profile or samples show they actually write this way. The profile always wins.

## Openers and closers
- Restating the request: "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I hope this email finds you well."
- Announcing the answer: "Here's a breakdown", "Let's dive in", "In this post, we'll explore…"
- Closing offers: "Let me know if you have any questions!", "Feel free to reach out", "Happy to help!"
- Summary sign-offs: "In summary", "Overall", "Ultimately", "At the end of the day"

## Vocabulary
- Inflated verbs: delve, leverage, utilize, harness, foster, bolster, streamline, elevate, empower, unlock, navigate (used figuratively)
- Inflated nouns and adjectives: tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, journey, robust, seamless, pivotal, crucial, comprehensive, cutting-edge, game-changer
- Filler intensifiers: truly, incredibly, absolutely, genuinely, deeply
- Corporate hedging: "it's worth noting that", "it's important to remember", "arguably"

## Structure
- Rule of three everywhere ("fast, reliable, and scalable")
- "It's not X, it's Y" and "Not only X, but also Y" constructions
- Every paragraph the same length, and every list exactly 3–5 bullets
- Bold lead-ins on every bullet when the user writes plain lists
- Headers on a message that's three paragraphs long
- Ending on a neat one-line moral or rhetorical question

## Punctuation and formatting
- Em dashes (—) when the user doesn't use them
- Emoji as bullet points
- Title Case In Headings when the user uses sentence case
- Colons introducing every list

## Tone
- Relentless positivity and enthusiasm the user didn't express
- Over-apologising, or over-thanking ("Thank you so much for your patience!")
- Hedging every claim, or no hedging at all when the user normally hedges
- Explaining things the recipient obviously knows
